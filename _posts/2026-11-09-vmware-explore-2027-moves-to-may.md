For as long as I've been going to VMworld (sorry, VMware Explore. I'm still not used to it), the conference has lived in the same spot on the calendar: the last week of August, right before Labor Day. And for just as long, that week has come with a ritual in my house. Pack the bag, print the session schedule, and then spend the whole flight wondering whether the kids made it onto the bus OK on their first days of school.

Broadcom just moved the whole thing.

According to the [save-the-date post on the VMware Explore blog](https://blogs.vmware.com/explore/save-the-date-2027/), VMware Explore 2027 is now set for **May 10-13, 2027** at **Resorts World Las Vegas**. No more late-August Vegas. No more back-to-school collision.

Just a different collision.

## The Details

Here's what the page lays out, minus the marketing:

- **When:** May 10-13, 2027 (Monday through Thursday)
- **Where:** Resorts World Las Vegas, at the north end of the Strip. The page calls out direct access to the Vegas Loop, the underground tunnel shuttle, for getting to other properties.
- **Call for Proposals:** Opened November 3, 2026. If you've been sitting on a session idea, the "Apply to Speak" link is on the save-the-date page.
- **Registration:** Official registration opens **January 12, 2027**.
- **Pre-registration discount:** There was a $100 discount on a Full Event Pass for pre-registering, but that window closed September 30, 2026. If you missed it, so did I.

What the page doesn't tell you yet: pass pricing, hotel rates or room blocks, keynote speakers, or a session catalog beyond four broad topic areas. Expect those closer to the January registration opening.

## What's Being Offered

The offerings won't surprise anyone who has been to one of these. The page lists four:

**Sessions.** Practitioner-focused deep dives across four areas: Cloud Infrastructure, Security, App Modernization, and Innovation. "Innovation" is carrying a lot of weight in that list, and I'd bet a decent chunk of it ends up being private AI on VCF.

**Hands-on Labs.** Self-paced and guided labs for testing private cloud solutions. I've built HOL content, so I'm biased, but this is still the best use of your time at the show. Sessions get posted online afterward. Sitting in a lab with an expert one aisle over does not.

**Certifications.** Accelerated onsite exams. If your employer is paying for the trip anyway, knocking out a cert while you're there is the cheapest one you'll ever get.

**Expert access.** One-on-one time with the architects and engineers who design VMware Cloud Foundation and the private AI pieces. This one is underrated. Bring a real problem from a real environment, not a "what's on the roadmap" question they can't answer anyway.

## Why May? Broadcom's Reasoning

The page gives four reasons for the move, and I'll give them their due:

1. **More runway to use what you learn.** Attending in May leaves you about seven months before year-end to put it to work, instead of roughly four. For anyone trying to get budget approved off the back of something they saw at the show, that's a real argument.
2. **No August heat.** The page cites "100°+" temperatures. Anyone who has walked from a Strip hotel to a convention hall in late August knows that number is generous.
3. **Late summer stays free.** Family vacations and back-to-school planning no longer compete with the conference.
4. **A tighter venue.** Shorter walks between rooms, sessions, and networking areas. I'll believe it when my feet do.

Reason #3 is the one I want to talk about.

## Real Talk: You Just Moved the Conflict

Broadcom is right that late August was rough on families. Kids starting school, schedules resetting, one parent flying to Vegas while the other one handles the first-week chaos solo. I've been the parent on the plane. It's not a great feeling.

But May isn't empty on a family calendar. May is graduation season.

College commencements cluster in early and mid May. A lot of high school graduations land in late May and into June. Then add end-of-year concerts, award nights, recitals, and finals. If you've got a kid finishing anything in spring 2027, there's a real chance that the conference and the ceremony are fighting over the same week. And unlike the first day of school, you don't get a do-over on a graduation.

Then there's the detail nobody seems to have checked: **May 9, 2027 is Mother's Day.** The conference starts Monday, May 10. So for a lot of attendees, the travel day to Las Vegas is Mother's Day. (I'll let you decide how you want to explain that one at home. I'm not volunteering.)

To be fair, there is no week of the year that works for every family. Every slot conflicts with something. Broadcom didn't eliminate the family conflict. They moved it from "will my kid get on the bus OK?" to "will I make it to my kid's graduation?" Which of those is worse depends on what your house looks like next spring.

## TIP: Check Your Calendar Now

If you're planning to go, here's what I'd do this week, not in January:

- **Get the school calendars.** Look up graduation, commencement, and end-of-year event dates for every kid in your house (and nieces, nephews, anyone you'd hate to miss). Many schools publish these a year out.
- **Watch the travel days.** A Monday start means Sunday travel. That Sunday is Mother's Day. Plan for it.
- **Decide before registration opens on January 12.** If your employer is paying, getting the conflict on the table before the purchase request goes in beats explaining it after the pass is bought.
- **Submit to the CFP if you've got something.** It opened November 3. Speakers have even less flexibility on dates than attendees do, so know your May calendar before you commit to presenting.

## Summary

VMware Explore 2027 runs May 10-13 at Resorts World Las Vegas. The CFP is open now, and registration opens January 12. The lineup of sessions, labs, certifications, and expert time is the familiar formula. The spring dates do have real upside: cooler weather, more of the fiscal year left to act on what you learn, and late summer back with the family.

I just hope nobody on the planning committee has a kid walking across a stage that week.
